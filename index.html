About Me

I build humane systems at the edge of science and AI.

Hello! I'm Olga Moreira.

I hold a PhD and MSc in Astrophysics from the University of Liège, and a BSc and MSc in Physics and Applied Mathematics (Astronomy) from the University of Porto.

Based in the Niagara region, I now focus on auditable, local-first, modular, and decentralized AI systems, as well as inclusive science outreach that amplifies underrepresented perspectives. My work centers on building technologies that are open, accessible, and sustainable, prioritizing user autonomy, resource efficiency, and reduced reliance on energy-intensive infrastructure.

Rooted in collaborations at the European Space Research and Technology Centre (ESTEC), the European Southern Observatory (ESO), and confidential consultancy roles, my career has evolved in parallel with the active deployment of my organization, OM Synaptic Systems.

On the education side, I am currently instructing undergraduate courses in Introduction to Physics, Introduction to Astronomy, and Foundations of Natural Science at the Newlane University.

Open Science Engagement

The scientific method and critical thinking should be harnessed as instruments of collective liberation, not hoarded as means of exclusion by those who weaponize knowledge to uphold elitist hierarchies.

As a researcher and open science advocate, I prioritize making science accessible to all. I collaborate with educators, developers, and communities to design tools and practices that democratize scientific knowledge, ensuring it serves as a shared resource rather than a gatekept privilege.

Core Commitments:

I align my work with the principles set out by the TU/e Code of Scientific Conduct and IGDORE's Open Scientific Practices, integrating them into every stage of my research, writing, and project design.

  • Integrity and Transparency — Upholding clarity in data, sources, and authorship.
  • Openness and Accessibility — Sharing knowledge responsibly, making science understandable and available to all.
  • Equity and Responsibility — Recognizing the social impact of research and prioritizing inclusivity in scientific participation.
  • Reflection and Accountability — Continuously examining how methods, technologies, and biases shape the stories we tell through science.

OM OpenSkies Initiative

I'm developing the OM OpenSkies Initiative, a long-term project to create a global platform for open-access astrophysics research, modern science education, and inclusive storytelling. It will create emotionally supportive, culturally inclusive scientific resources and integrate Indigenous sky traditions and marginalized voices into a shared vision of the cosmos. Planned projects include the OM Atlas of the Skies, Silenced Voices in Science, and the SkyMemory Archive. The initiative is grounded in the idea that science is a shared right, not a privilege.

Solar Physics Research

My doctoral research explored the seismic rhythms of red giants and the precision of interferometry. However, it was my early studies in astro-MHD that first ignited my passion for astrophysics. While I no longer focus on asteroseismology, analytical and computational tools I developed continue to inform my independent research on solar dynamics, flare statistics, and the interplay between magnetic activity in the Sun and other stars. Today, I'm particularly excited about integrating MHD simulations with machine learning to advance data-driven heliophysics, a field that bridges the elegance of classical plasma physics with the transformative potential of modern AI.
